homdu app Privacy Policy.

Effective date: 5 October 2026 Last updated: 6 October 2026

This Privacy Policy describes how the personal data of users of the homdu app (the "App") — a building and renovation journal — is processed. It explains what data is processed, for what purpose, on what legal basis, where it is located, who receives it and what rights users have.

Version: 2.0 — applies to the homdu App version 2.0 and later. Replaces: the Privacy Policy of 22 June 2026, which applied to versions 1.x of the App — previous version.

Summary of changes in version 2.0

Compared with the previous version:

  1. Project sharing — we describe what data invited people see, how permissions work and how sharing ends (sections 4.2 and 7.3).
  2. Files in iCloud — expense attachments, covers, backgrounds and album photos now go to iCloud as well (sections 4.1 and 7.2).
  3. Demo mode — the sample Project's data is stored on the device; the App does not send it to iCloud or to any server (sections 4.7 and 7.1).
  4. Two separate consent categories — "Analytics and diagnostics" (Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics) and "Marketing" (Meta), with a description of the identifiers involved, including the IDFA (section 9).
  5. Account — signing in with "Sign in with Apple" is required to create your own Projects and to synchronise (section 8).
  6. Notifications — we describe the registration of the device on our backend, which takes place when the App is launched, also without an Account (sections 4.5 and 7.4).
  7. Account deletion — we describe what the "Delete account" feature deletes, what it does not delete and how to complete the deletion (section 8).
  8. Document layout — we added "what data / why / legal basis / recipient / how long" tables and a clear split between the device, iCloud, our backend and third parties (sections 3 and 4).
  9. Legal bases and retention periods — registering the device and sending notifications from our backend now rely on legitimate interest, and service messages on the contract (previously: system permission for notifications, and the contract); crash reports rely on consent only (previously: consent and legitimate interest); content and the referral programme rely on the contract (previously: the contract and legitimate interest). The device record and referral programme data are kept with no automatic time limit, until deleted at your request (sections 4.5, 4.8, 6, 12 and 17).

02Definitions

  • App – the homdu app for iPhone and iPad, made available on the App Store.
  • User ("you") – a natural person using the App.
  • Account – the User's account in the App, created by signing in with "Sign in with Apple".
  • Personal data – information about an identified or identifiable natural person.
  • Project – a building or renovation project run in the App, together with the content assigned to it.
  • Project Owner – the User who created a Project and may share it with other people.
  • Collaborator – a person who has accepted an invitation to a Shared Project (a share participant).
  • Shared Project – a Project shared with Collaborators through Apple's CloudKit service.
  • Demo mode – using the sample Project generated in the App; it is available without an Account, and a signed-in User can switch the sample Project on in the App's Settings.
  • iCloud / CloudKit – Apple services for storing and synchronising data in the User's Apple account.
  • Backend – the Controller's homdu-api service: code running with the hosting provider Vercel and a Google Cloud Firestore database.
  • Device – the device on which the App is installed.
  • IDFA – the advertising identifier of an Apple device.
  • IDFV – the identifier for vendor: an identifier assigned to the Device for the apps of a single publisher.
  • ATT – the iOS system prompt asking for permission to track (App Tracking Transparency).
  • Processor – an entity processing data on behalf of and on the instructions of the Controller.
  • EEA – the European Economic Area.

07Where the data is located

7.1. On your Device (area A)

Project content, document files, journal entry photos and the data from section 4.9 are saved in the App's storage on the Device. App settings and the saved consent choice are saved in the standard system mechanisms. The Apple user identifier and — if Apple passed them — the name and email address are saved in the Device keychain; this entry is not carried over to other devices.

Demo mode. The sample Project is generated in the App and stored locally. The App does not send its data to iCloud or to any server.

Data stored only on the Device does not reach the Controller.

Device backup. App data saved on the Device may be included in the Device backup made by iOS (in iCloud or on a computer), if you use one — the App does not exclude it from that backup. This also applies to data described in this Policy as stored on the Device (Demo mode, files from the "Documents" tab, journal entry photos, the data from section 4.9). You manage the backup in iOS settings; a backup made earlier may contain this data even after you use "Delete account" or remove the App. The Controller has no access to backups.

7.2. In your iCloud — synchronisation (area B)

After you sign in, the App saves the data listed in section 4.1 in the private iCloud database in your Apple account. If you had your own Projects on the Device before signing in, they are sent to that database at the first sign-in.

Text from receipt photos is recognised on the Device (OCR).

Deleting content. Deleting a Project, a stage or an expense in the App does not always delete all related data and files from iCloud — for example, the tasks, expenses and contacts of a deleted stage, or the attachment file of a deleted expense, may remain in iCloud. How to delete the remaining App data from iCloud is described in section 8.6.

This data is stored and processed by Apple under the terms of the iCloud service. The Controller as a company has no access to it and cannot read, change or delete it at your request — you manage it in the App and in your Apple account settings.

7.3. Project sharing (area B)

Invitation. The Project Owner invites other people in Apple's system share sheet — with a link, through the Messages app or by another route chosen there. The recipient is selected in Apple's interface; the App does not process the invited person's data and the Controller does not receive it. The invitation contains the Project name. Only invited people have access.

Accepting an invitation. Accepting an invitation requires an iCloud account on the device. Ongoing synchronisation of changes requires signing in to the App.

What invited people see. Collaborators receive the Shared Project data listed in section 4.2. This includes third-party data that the Project Owner entered in the Project — in particular contractors' contacts with phone numbers, email addresses, ratings and notes.

Participants. The list of participants is kept and displayed by Apple in the system share sheet. Apple may record with data in iCloud a technical identifier of the account of the person who created or changed it; the App does not read it and does not show who added an expense or an entry.

Permissions. The Collaborator's permission level (view or edit) is set by the Project Owner in Apple's system sheet. The App applies these settings.

Ending. A Collaborator can leave the share. The Project Owner can end it. The Shared Project's data stays in the Project Owner's private iCloud database. Deleting a Shared Project by the Project Owner does not end the share and does not delete all of its data from the Project Owner's iCloud — the remaining data may still be accessible to Collaborators; the Project Owner should end the share before deleting a Shared Project.

Local copies. After a share is left or ended, a copy of the data may remain on the Collaborator's Device until it is removed by the App or by the Collaborator. The Controller cannot remove such copies remotely.

A Collaborator's entries. Expenses, entries and files added by a Collaborator become part of the Project Owner's Project and stay in the Owner's iCloud — also after the Collaborator leaves the share.

The roles of Apple and the Controller. Shared data is stored and processed by Apple within iCloud and is visible to the people chosen by the Project Owner. The Controller has no access to it. The events described in sections 4.3 and 4.4 (e.g. rating a contractor, with the rating value) are generated on your Device also when you work in a Shared Project and, after consents are saved, go to Google and Meta for the purposes described in those sections. Apart from these events, the Controller does not use shared data for advertising or profiling.

The Project Owner's responsibility. The Project Owner decides who the Project is shared with and is responsible for that choice. The Owner should share it only with people to whom they are allowed to disclose the information it contains — in particular contractors' contact details and ratings, photos of people, and receipts and other expense attachments containing personal data.

7.4. On our Backend (area C)

The following reaches the Backend:

  • the Device record for notifications — when the App is launched, also without an Account (section 4.5);
  • referral programme data — when the App language is Polish (section 4.8);
  • technical request data and guide search phrases (section 4.8).

Files, data from the finance form, and the name and email address from Apple sign-in are not sent to the Backend. From Project content, only the key of the stage for which the App fetches guides and partner content reaches the Backend (section 4.8): for stages from the App's template this is a fixed template key, and for other stages — in particular those you created yourself — the stage's name in simplified form (lower case, without Polish diacritics, underscores instead of spaces).

The Backend code runs with the hosting provider Vercel. Device records and referral programme data are saved in a Google Cloud Firestore database located in Europe. The notification token is also processed by Google (Firebase Cloud Messaging) and Apple (APNs) to the extent needed to deliver a notification; this does not depend on the consents described in section 9.

Guide content and partner content come from the Sanity content management system. The App fetches text through the Backend; images (banners, partner logos, covers) — directly from Sanity's servers (section 10.5).

7.5. With third parties for analytics and marketing — after consent is given (area D)

After the "Analytics" consent is saved, the data described in section 4.3 goes to Google. After the "Marketing" consent is saved, the data described in section 4.4 goes to Meta. Section 9 contains the details. This does not apply to notification delivery (section 4.5) or to the configuration connection of the Meta SDK (section 9.3).

08Account and Account deletion

8.1. Signing in takes place through "Sign in with Apple". It is required to create your own Projects and to synchronise. Without an Account you can use Demo mode.

8.2. In the Device keychain we store the Apple user identifier and — if Apple passes them at the first sign-in — the name and email address. You can use Apple's "Hide My Email" feature; the App then receives a relay address from Apple.

8.3. For the sign-in service, Apple acts as a separate data controller. The processing rules are set out in Apple's privacy policy.

8.4. What the "Delete account" feature does. The feature is available in the App's Settings under the name "Delete account and all data". Using it:

  • marks the Device record on the Backend as inactive;
  • deletes from your private iCloud database the Project content saved by the App: Projects, journal entries, stages, tasks, expenses, stage budgets, contacts, file descriptions, links, albums, events, the document descriptions saved by earlier versions, and expense attachments saved in the older format (by earlier versions or at the first sign-in) together with the text recognised from them (OCR);
  • deletes App data saved on the Device, including Demo mode data and the data from section 4.9;
  • deletes the keychain entries, signs you out and deletes the saved consent choice.

8.5. What the "Delete account" feature does not delete.

  • In iCloud: files sent by the App in the current format (expense attachments, covers, backgrounds, album photos) and the Project budget; Shared Project data — the feature does not end shares you own or leave shares in which you are a Collaborator.
  • On the Device: files from the "Documents" tab, journal entry photos and some technical settings, including the saved referral programme profile with its identifier. This data disappears when the App is removed from the Device.
  • On the Backend: the Device record (it remains as inactive, with the token and identifier) and referral programme data. At the next launch of the App the Device may be registered again, without a user identifier.
  • At Apple: the link between the App and your Apple account in "Sign in with Apple".
  • At Google and Meta: data passed earlier on the basis of consents; the feature switches off further collection but does not change the identifiers assigned by the SDKs.

If deletion is interrupted (e.g. no connection or iCloud unavailable), some data in iCloud may already be deleted while the data on the Device and the Account remain; the feature then has to be run again.

8.6. How to complete the deletion.

  • Data in iCloud: before deleting the Account, end the shares you own and leave those in which you are a Collaborator. You can delete the remaining App data in the iCloud settings on the device (manage account storage → homdu → delete data).
  • Data on the Device: remove the App from the Device.
  • Data on the Backend: write to homdu.app@gmail.com. 09Consents: analytics and marketing

9.1. Consent screen. At first launch — and once after updating from version 1.x — the App shows a screen with two categories: "Analytics and diagnostics" and "Marketing". The choice is saved on the Device and does not reach our Backend. Information that settings were saved in the "Cookie settings" sheet (the state of the switches) may be passed to Google as an analytics event under the "Analytics" category (section 4.3).

9.2. The "Analytics" consent. Once it is saved, the App switches on collection in Google services:

  • Firebase Analytics — App usage events, installation identifier, identifier for vendor (IDFV), Device and system data; if you allowed tracking in the ATT prompt — also the IDFA;
  • Firebase Crashlytics — crash and error reports.

We do not attribute events to your Account or user identifier. Google signals (linking data with Google accounts) is switched off.

9.3. The "Marketing" consent. Once it is saved, the App passes conversion events to Meta through the Meta SDK (Facebook SDK): installation and launch of the App, and selected product events. Our purpose is to measure the effectiveness of the App's advertising. Meta also uses these events for the purposes set out in section 9.6. Events reach Meta from the App only; our Backend does not pass data to Meta.

The IDFA is passed to Meta only when both of the following apply:

  1. you saved the "Marketing" consent in the App, and
  2. you allowed tracking in the iOS system prompt (ATT).

The ATT prompt appears only after a choice is saved with the "Marketing" category switched on. Withdrawing either of these consents stops the IDFA from being passed to Meta. Withdrawing the "Marketing" consent stops events from being passed to Meta.

Configuration connection. Regardless of consent, the Meta SDK fetches its configuration from Meta's servers when the App is launched. Meta then receives the Device's IP address, the App identifier, and the system and SDK version. We do not pass conversion events or the IDFA before a consent is saved.

9.4. How to withdraw consent.

  • "Analytics" and "Marketing" — Settings → "Consent & privacy" → "Cookie settings";
  • permission to track (ATT) — in iOS system settings: Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

Withdrawing consent does not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out before the withdrawal. After you withdraw a consent, the App switches off collection in that category; the identifiers assigned by the SDKs are not changed in the process. For data passed earlier, you can ask for it to be erased (section 17.3) as described in section 8.6: we do not attribute events to the Account, so without additional information we cannot single out a specific person's data. Analytics data and crash reports in Google's services expire within the periods given in section 12; data passed to Meta is kept and deleted under Meta's rules. To the extent Meta processes the data as a separate controller, you can also send the request to Meta.

9.5. Legal basis. The legal basis for processing in both categories is consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). The consent also covers access to information stored on the Device and storing identifiers on it to the extent these services need (Article 399 of the Polish Electronic Communications Law).
